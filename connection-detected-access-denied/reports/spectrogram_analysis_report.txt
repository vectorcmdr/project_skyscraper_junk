================================================================================
QUALITATIVE OBSERVATIONS
================================================================================

1. Row and column scans produce the longest audio (~36 s) because every pixel
   is used exactly once. The spectrograms reveal strong horizontal or vertical
   stripe artifacts corresponding to the image's spatial periodicity.

2. Diagonal and zigzag scans also use all pixels once but reorder them,
   which tends to smear spatial periodicity into broadband noise in the
   spectrogram, lowering the dominance of discrete harmonic structures.

3. Griffin-Lim reconstructions are shorter (~18 s) because the STFT overlap
   reduces the effective sample count relative to frame count.
   They attempt to recover a coherent time-domain signal from the image's
   magnitude structure; the resulting spectrograms show energy concentrated
   in bands that mirror the original image's luminance gradients.

4. Rotating the image swaps the roles of rows and columns. Consequently,
   row-scan spectrograms of the rotated images resemble column-scan
   spectrograms of the original, and vice-versa.

5. Because the source image contains sharp edges, the raw pixel scans contain
   many abrupt 0->255 transitions. These create broad-spectrum impulsive noise
   in the resulting audio and spectrograms. Pop-rocks for your ears!
